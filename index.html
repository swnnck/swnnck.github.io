Privacy Persona



Learn about your internet privacy knowledge on an adventure-based story quiz inspired by Cosmos Persona and Cake Resume


Made by Sam Winnick - Undergraduate Capstone Project


The Personas

About the Project

The necessity to increase digital literacy, particularly internet privacy awareness in order to safely navigate the web has become imperative. Privacy Persona, a quiz that assesses user’s knowledge on internet privacy skills, works to confront these increasing concerns. Following the user through a narrative adventure acting as an allegory for the web, allows for users to explore their online habits through self-assessment.

Method

To situate the project within the digital humanities, it was required to have in-depth background knowledge of self assessment, social categorization theory, the rise and impact of quiz taking, interactive learning, and the relationship individuals develop with digital spaces. Similar studies that explore web user archetypes such as Charset and Bedard's1, Meyen et al.'s2 and Dehling et al.'s3 created a foundation for preexisting scholarship and helped develop the quiz results. Using the existing typologies in research and comparing their traits, I developed my persona results and alongside wrote the questionnaire for the quiz using studies highlighting the most common uncertainties and skill gaps in digital literacy. The project was coded on Visual Studio code using JavaScript, HTML/CSS and debugged with Claude AI. The quiz is currently hosted on Github pages for open public access.

Deliverable

The influence of self-assessment and social categorization became a widely shared sentiment across the early age of internet. Internet groups were formed based on common interests, such as fandoms, hobbies, lifestyle, and more. Thus came the emergence of grouping and association on the internet and along came the construction of digital identities (Berberick and McAllister 3424)4. The desire to relate to others and find connections through the internet would inevitably impact the influence of personality quizzes finding their popularity.
••• In the early 2010s, BuzzFeed became widely known for their creative utilization of personality quizzes. It became a popular model for their website to gain user interaction by catering to the digital self-construction (Berberick and McAllister 3435) and self-categorization motivations (Hornsey 215)5. With the ability to create your own personality quiz, from topics ranging to “what character are you?” to “what food item are you?”, BuzzFeed monetized pre-existing digital behaviours such as finding community, building upon ones digital identity, and fan culture, to categorize and unite web users with similar traits, behaviours, and interests.
••• In 2024 , playful, artistic personality quizzes began to circulate on GitHub. Artists began designing, programming, and adding unique visuals and themes to the quizzes. Cosmos Persona6, developed by I-see Warisa Jaidee, went viral for its narrative storytelling and unique graphics, situating itself between play, art, and learning. Cake Resume7 was created by an online hub for resume building and job searching, which encouraged users to learn about their career habits through a fun, engaging, pastry-themed design. This was the first viral instance where quiz-taking methods were used for educational purposes outside of psychology, providing users with insights to improve their interview skills. The natural shareability of quiz content as discussed by Berberick and McAllister allows for these projects to reach many internet users and act as a means of self-promotion for the project itself, encouraging discourse and topic awareness.
